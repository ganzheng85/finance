# Historical Dip Buying Examples

Real-world case studies of bargains (successful dip buys) vs value traps (failed dip buys).

## BARGAINS (Successful Dip Buys)

### Example 1: Meta Platforms (META) - Q4 2022 Drop

**Situation**:
- **Date**: November 2022 - February 2023
- **Price**: $145 → $90 (-38% drop)
- **Catalyst**: Q4 2022 earnings miss, metaverse losses ballooning to $13B/year, TikTok threat, recession fears

**Bargain Test Score: 4.5/5** ✅

1. **Business Intact?** ✅ PASS
   - Moat: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp = 3B+ users, dominant in social. Network effects intact.
   - Revenue: Ad business slowing (recession), but not structurally broken. Q4 revenue +4% YoY.
   - Margins: Compressed to 20% (from 40%) due to metaverse R&D + efficiency losses. Fixable.
   - Balance sheet: $40B cash, zero debt. Fortress.

2. **Management Credible?** ⚠️ CAUTION
   - Track record: Zuckerberg built FB, acquired Instagram, WhatsApp. Proven product visionary.
   - Transparency: Admitted "year of efficiency" needed, announced layoffs (11K people, 13% of workforce).
   - Action plan: Cut metaverse spending, focus on AI, improve Reels monetization. Credible.
   - Insiders: Zuckerberg owns 13% ($30B+ stake), not selling. Aligned.

3. **Valuation Compelling?** ✅ PASS
   - P/E: 10x (vs history 20-30x, peers 15-20x). Market pricing in permanent margin collapse.
   - DCF fair value: $150-180 (assuming margins recover to 30-35%). Current $90 = 40-50% discount.
   - Margin of safety: 40-50% (STRONG PASS).

4. **Risk-Reward?** ✅ PASS
   - Bull case: +67-100% to $150-180 if "year of efficiency" works (Prob: 60%)
   - Base case: +30-50% to $120-135 if modest recovery (Prob: 30%)
   - Bear case: -20-30% to $60-70 if ad recession deepens, TikTok wins (Prob: 10%)
   - Asymmetry: 4:1 upside/downside (STRONG PASS).

5. **Catalyst?** ✅ PASS
   - Near-term: Q1 2023 earnings (April) will show if cost cuts + Reels monetization working.
   - Medium-term: AI integration (Llama LLMs), ad targeting improvements.
   - Timing: 3-6 months. Probability: High (Zuck executes on efficiency).

**Entry Strategy**:
- Entry 1 (33%): $88-92 after stabilization (bought $90, 1.5% portfolio)
- Entry 2 (33%): $102 after +12% bounce confirmed trend reversal (1.5% portfolio)
- Entry 3 (33%): $115 after Q1 2023 earnings beat (1.5% portfolio)
- **Total position**: 4.5% portfolio, avg cost $102

**Outcome**:
- Q1 2023 (April 26, 2023): Beat expectations, stock rallies to $220 (+144% from $90 low)
- Exit plan:
  - Sold 25% at $140 (+37% from avg cost) - locked in quick win
  - Sold 50% at $180 (+76% from avg cost) - fair value reached
  - Held 25% with trailing stop-loss (20% from peak)
- Meta hit $475 in late 2024 (+428% from $90 low)

**Lesson**: Temporary fear (metaverse losses, TikTok hype) + intact moat (3B users, network effects) + fortress balance sheet + credible management turnaround = textbook bargain.

**Why It Worked**:
1. Market overreacted to metaverse losses (real issue, but not existential)
2. Core ad business was slowing (recession), not broken
3. Zuckerberg's track record of executing turnarounds (Instagram Reels beat TikTok)
4. Valuation became absurd (10x P/E for a cash-rich, moat-protected business)

### Example 2: NVIDIA (NVDA) - Crypto Crash 2018

**Situation**:
- **Date**: October 2018 - December 2018
- **Price**: $290 → $127 (-56% drop)
- **Catalyst**: Crypto crash (GPU demand for mining evaporated), inventory glut, China slowdown

**Bargain Test Score: 4/5** ✅

1. **Business Intact?** ✅ PASS
   - Moat: CUDA software platform, AI/datacenter market leadership. Switching costs high.
   - Revenue: Gaming (crypto-driven) collapsing, but datacenter +50% YoY (AI training demand).
   - Margins: 60% gross margins intact (not crypto-dependent).
   - Balance sheet: $7B cash, $2B debt. Strong.

2. **Management Credible?** ✅ PASS
   - Track record: Jensen Huang pivoted NVDA from gaming → AI/datacenter (visionary).
   - Transparency: Admitted crypto demand was "pulled forward" (honest assessment).
   - Action plan: Cut GPU production, focus on datacenter, invest in AI software.
   - Insiders: Huang owns 4%, not selling.

3. **Valuation Compelling?** ✅ PASS
   - P/E: 18x (vs history 30-40x). Market pricing in permanent gaming decline.
   - Fair value: $200-250 (based on datacenter growth). Current $127 = 40-50% discount.
   - Margin of safety: 40%+.

4. **Risk-Reward?** ✅ PASS
   - Bull case: +100%+ if datacenter + AI trends accelerate (Prob: 50%)
   - Base case: +50-70% if crypto stabilizes, gaming recovers (Prob: 40%)
   - Bear case: -25% if datacenter slows (Prob: 10%)
   - Asymmetry: 4:1+.

5. **Catalyst?** ⚠️ CAUTION
   - Near-term: Inventory glut takes 6-9 months to clear (slow).
   - Medium-term: Datacenter revenue growth will become obvious in 2019-20 earnings.
   - Timing: 6-12 months. Requires patience.

**Entry Strategy**:
- Entry 1: $130-140 after initial stabilization (2% portfolio)
- Entry 2: $160 after bounce confirmed (1.5% portfolio)
- Entry 3: Held off (waited for inventory clarity)
- **Total**: 3.5% portfolio, avg cost $143

**Outcome**:
- 2019: Stock recovered to $250 (+97% from $127 low)
- 2020: COVID AI boom → $540 (+325% from low)
- 2021-24: AI revolution → $900+ (+608% from low)

**Lesson**: Temporary headwind (crypto crash) + intact secular trend (AI/datacenter) + management pivoting correctly = massive long-term winner.

**Why It Worked**:
1. Crypto was <20% of revenue; market treated it as 100% (overreaction)
2. Datacenter/AI was the real story (50% growth), ignored during panic
3. CUDA moat meant NVDA was irreplaceable for AI training
4. Patience required (took 9 months to work), but asymmetry was huge

### Example 3: American Express (AXP) - Financial Crisis 2009

**Situation**:
- **Date**: March 2009
- **Price**: $50 → $10 (-80% drop)
- **Catalyst**: Financial crisis, credit losses spiking, Warren Buffett doubled down

**Bargain Test Score: 3.5/5** ⚠️ (High risk, but compelling for risk-takers)

1. **Business Intact?** ⚠️ CAUTION
   - Moat: Amex brand, affluent customer base (less cyclical than competitors). Network effects.
   - Revenue: Contracting (recession), but Amex customers have lower default rates than Visa/MC.
   - Margins: Compressed (credit losses), but historically recover post-recession.
   - Balance sheet: Strained (needed $3B TARP funds), but not insolvent.

2. **Management Credible?** ✅ PASS
   - Track record: Ken Chenault navigated 9/11, tech bubble. Proven crisis manager.
   - Transparency: Candid about credit losses, conservative guidance.
   - Action plan: Cut costs, tighten credit standards, accepted TARP to shore up capital.
   - Buffett: Doubled down (bought $300M more). Signal of confidence.

3. **Valuation Compelling?** ✅ PASS
   - P/E: N/A (losses due to credit provisions). P/B: 0.8x (below tangible book).
   - Fair value: $25-35 (normalized earnings). Current $10 = 60-70% discount.
   - Margin of safety: 60%+ (if survives).

4. **Risk-Reward?** ✅ PASS
   - Bull case: +150-250% to $25-35 if recession ends, credit normalizes (Prob: 60%)
   - Base case: +50-100% to $15-20 if slow recovery (Prob: 30%)
   - Bear case: -50-80% to $2-5 if depression, bankruptcy (Prob: 10%)
   - Asymmetry: 3:1+ (but tail risk of wipeout).

5. **Catalyst?** ⚠️ CAUTION
   - Near-term: TARP repayment signals strength (12-18 months).
   - Medium-term: Credit losses peak, then normalize (24 months).
   - Timing: Long (2-3 years). Requires stomach for volatility.

**Entry Strategy**:
- Entry 1: $12-15 after March 2009 lows (high risk, 2% portfolio)
- Entry 2: Did NOT add (too much uncertainty, preserved capital)
- **Total**: 2% portfolio, avg cost $13

**Outcome**:
- 2010: Stock at $40 (+200% from $10 low) as credit losses peaked
- 2013: Stock at $70 (+600% from low) as economy normalized
- 2017-21: Stock at $120-150 (+1,100-1,400% from low)

**Lesson**: Crisis creates extreme fear → extreme mispricing. Buffett's endorsement was catalyst.

**Why It Worked**:
1. Market priced in bankruptcy (P/B 0.8x). Amex wasn't going bankrupt (TARP backstop).
2. Affluent customer base meant credit losses were temporary, not structural.
3. Buffett's vote of confidence ($300M investment) signaled bottom.
4. Patience required (took 2-3 years), but asymmetry was exceptional (10-14x).

## VALUE TRAPS (Failed Dip Buys)

### Example 1: General Electric (GE) - 2017-2020 Collapse

**Situation**:
- **Date**: 2017-2020
- **Price**: $30 → $20 → $12 → $6 (-80% total drop)
- **Catalyst**: Dividend cut, insurance losses, power business collapse, accounting concerns

**Bargain Test Score: 1.5/5** ❌ VALUE TRAP

1. **Business Intact?** ❌ FAIL
   - Moat: Eroding. Power division (gas turbines) losing to renewables (wind/solar).
   - Revenue: Declining across multiple divisions (Power, Aviation pre-COVID).
   - Margins: Compressing. Insurance liabilities ballooning ($15B+ shortfall).
   - Balance sheet: $115B debt. Overleveraged. Credit rating cut to near-junk.

2. **Management Credible?** ❌ FAIL
   - Track record: Jeff Immelt (CEO 2001-17) hid problems for years. New CEO Flannery fired after 14 months.
   - Transparency: Immelt denied problems ("dividend is safe"). Proved false (cut 50% in Nov 2017).
   - Action plan: Flannery's plan (sell assets, pay down debt) was too late. Larry Culp (2018) inherited mess.
   - Insiders: Selling heavily (red flag).

3. **Valuation Compelling?** ⚠️ CAUTION (Cheap, but for good reason)
   - P/E: 15x at $30 (seemed cheap vs history 18-22x). But earnings were fake (insurance losses hidden).
   - Fair value: $12-15 (after adjusting for debt, insurance liabilities). $30 was NOT cheap.
   - Margin of safety: Zero. Value was an illusion.

4. **Risk-Reward?** ❌ FAIL
   - Bull case: +30-50% if turnaround works (Prob: 20%)
   - Base case: -20-40% if muddles through (Prob: 50%)
   - Bear case: -60-80% if debt spiral, fire sale (Prob: 30%)
   - Asymmetry: <1:1 (MORE downside than upside).

5. **Catalyst?** ❌ FAIL
   - Near-term: No catalyst. Each earnings report brought new bad news (insurance, power write-downs).
   - Medium-term: Turnaround would take 5-10 years (too long, too uncertain).
   - Timing: N/A. No clear path.

**What Happened**:
- 2017: $30 → $20 (dividend cut #1)
- 2018: $20 → $12 (dividend cut #2, Flannery fired, Culp hired)
- 2019: $12 → $8 (power division implosion, $22B write-down)
- 2020: $8 → $6 (COVID, aviation collapse)

**Lesson**: "Cheap" P/E on fake earnings = value trap. Overleveraged balance sheet + broken business model + incompetent management = avoid.

**Why It Failed**:
1. **Moat was gone**: Power business disrupted by renewables. No competitive advantage left.
2. **Hidden liabilities**: $15B+ insurance shortfall not in earnings. Accounting gimmicks.
3. **Management in denial**: Immelt hid problems. Flannery fired too fast to fix. Culp inherited unfixable mess.
4. **Debt spiral**: $115B debt + declining cash flow = asset fire sales, value destruction.

**Red Flags Missed**:
- Dividend yield 5%+ (unsustainable, cut was obvious)
- CEO selling stock (insiders knew it was broken)
- Multiple division struggles (not one-off issue, systemic)
- Accounting complexity (conglomerate discount for a reason)

### Example 2: Boeing (BA) - 737 MAX Crisis 2019-2020

**Situation**:
- **Date**: March 2019 - March 2020
- **Price**: $440 → $375 (MAX grounding) → $95 (COVID)
- **Catalyst**: 737 MAX crashes (346 deaths), FAA grounding, then COVID hit

**Bargain Test Score: 2/5** ❌ VALUE TRAP (2019-20), Later ✅ BARGAIN (2021+)

**Phase 1 (2019): Premature Dip Buy - TRAP**

1. **Business Intact?** ⚠️ CAUTION (Seemed fixable, but wasn't)
   - Moat: Boeing/Airbus duopoly intact. Switching costs high (airlines can't switch).
   - Revenue: 737 MAX orders cancelled (lost $10B+), but backlog still large.
   - Margins: Crushed (787 production issues, 737 MAX costs).
   - Balance sheet: $27B debt (rising). Burned cash during grounding.

2. **Management Credible?** ❌ FAIL
   - Track record: CEO Muilenburg hid MCAS software issue. Fired December 2019.
   - Transparency: Denied safety issues, blamed pilots. Lost credibility with FAA.
   - Action plan: No clear path to re-certification (FAA trust broken).
   - Insiders: CEO fired. Red flag.

3. **Valuation Compelling?** ⚠️ CAUTION
   - P/E: N/A (losses). EV/EBITDA: 15x (vs history 12x). Not cheap enough for the risk.
   - Fair value: $300-350 IF MAX returns in 6 months. Uncertain.
   - Margin of safety: 10-20% (thin).

4. **Risk-Reward?** ❌ FAIL
   - Bull case: +20-30% if MAX returns Q4 2019 (Prob: 40%)
   - Base case: Flat to -10% if MAX delayed to 2020 (Prob: 40%)
   - Bear case: -30-50% if MAX permanently grounded (Prob: 20%)
   - Asymmetry: 1:1 (equal upside/downside).

5. **Catalyst?** ⚠️ CAUTION
   - Timing: FAA re-certification "soon" (management said Q4 2019). Proved false (took until Nov 2020).
   - Probability: Uncertain. FAA was furious, process opaque.

**Outcome (2019-20)**:
- MAX grounding extended to Nov 2020 (18 months, not 6)
- COVID hit March 2020: BA $375 → $95 (-75% from pre-MAX price $440)
- **Result**: Dip buyers at $375 lost -70-75% in 12 months. VALUE TRAP.

**Phase 2 (2021): Delayed Bargain - SUCCESS**

After MAX returned + vaccines → BA $95 → $265 (2021-24), +179%.

**Lesson**: Timing matters. 2019 dip buyers were too early (management lying, timeline uncertain). 2020 dip buyers (at $95-120 post-COVID) got the bargain (MAX returning, vaccines coming).

**Why Phase 1 Failed**:
1. **Management lied**: Muilenburg said "6 months to re-cert." Took 18 months. Trust broken.
2. **Regulatory risk underestimated**: FAA was furious (346 deaths). Approval not guaranteed.
3. **COVID black swan**: Even if MAX returned, COVID collapsed airline demand.
4. **Insufficient discount**: $375 was only -15% below fair value. Needed -40-50% for risk.

**Red Flags**:
- CEO fired (governance crisis)
- Criminal investigation (DOJ looking at MCAS cover-up)
- Regulatory uncertainty (FAA timeline opaque)
- Rising debt + cash burn (balance sheet stress)

3. **Valuation Compelling?** ❌ FAIL
   - IPO valuation: $47B (ridiculous). Post-collapse: $8B (still too high).

4. **Risk-Reward?** ❌ FAIL
   - Bull case: -50% (slow bleed to zero)

3. **Fraudulent CEO**: Neumann self-dealing, hyping "AI-powered community" (nonsense).
4. **Negative value**: Liabilities exceeded assets. Equity was worthless at ANY price.

## Key Takeaways: Bargain vs Trap

### Bargains Have:
1. ✅ **Intact moat** (competitive advantages preserved)
2. ✅ **Temporary problem** (earnings miss, macro selloff, not structural decline)
3. ✅ **Credible management** (track record of fixing issues, transparent)
4. ✅ **Compelling valuation** (30-50% below fair value, margin of safety)
5. ✅ **Catalyst for recovery** (within 3-12 months, high probability)

**Examples**: Meta 2022, NVDA 2018, AXP 2009

### Value Traps Have:
1. ❌ **Eroding moat** (competitive advantages weakening)
2. ❌ **Structural problem** (business model broken, secular decline)
3. ❌ **Incompetent/dishonest management** (deny issues, no plan, insiders selling)
4. ❌ **Fake valuation** (cheap P/E on fake earnings, hidden liabilities)
5. ❌ **No catalyst** (hope and prayers, 5-10 year "turnaround")

**Examples**: GE 2017-20, Boeing 2019-20 (premature), WeWork 2019-23

## Decision Framework

When you see a stock down -20-50%, ask:

**Is this Meta 2022 (BARGAIN) or GE 2017 (TRAP)?**

| Factor | Meta 2022 (BARGAIN) | GE 2017 (TRAP) |
|--------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Moat** | Intact (3B users, network effects) | Eroding (power disrupted by renewables) |
| **Problem** | Temporary (metaverse losses, recession) | Structural (debt spiral, division failures) |
| **Management** | Credible (Zuck track record, "year of efficiency") | Incompetent (Immelt hid problems, Flannery fired fast) |
| **Valuation** | Compelling (10x P/E, 40% discount) | Fake (15x P/E on fake earnings, hidden insurance losses) |
| **Catalyst** | Clear (Q1 2023 earnings, AI integration, 3-6mo) | None (each quarter brought new bad news) |
| **Outcome** | +428% over 2 years | -80% over 3 years |

**Action**: Run 5-Point Bargain Test. Score 4-5/5 = Buy. Score 0-2/5 = Avoid.
