# Dip Buying & Price Drop Trading Skill

A systematic approach to buying stocks or ETFs when prices drop, distinguishing between temporary selloffs (opportunities) and structural deterioration (value traps), with disciplined entry, sizing, and exit rules.

## Philosophy

**Core Principle**: "Buy low, sell high" sounds simple but requires discipline to execute. Most investors do the opposite - panic sell on drops, FOMO buy on rallies. This skill provides a framework to capitalize on fear-driven selloffs while avoiding falling knives.

**Key Insight**: Not all price drops are buying opportunities. You must distinguish:
- **Bargain** = Temporary drop, fundamentals intact, overreaction (BUY)
- **Value Trap** = Permanent impairment, fundamentals broken, justified decline (AVOID)

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Drop Detection & Triage (What Happened?)

1. **Quantify the Drop**
   - How much has it fallen? (%, $ amount, from what level?)
   - Over what timeframe? (1 day? 1 week? 1 month?)
   - From 52-week high or recent peak?
   - Current price vs 50-day, 200-day moving averages

2. **Identify the Catalyst**
   - Earnings miss or pre-announcement
   - Sector rotation or macro selloff
   - Company-specific news (management change, lawsuit, product failure)
   - Technical breakdown (broke support, stop-loss cascade)
   - No clear reason (general market weakness)

3. **Assess Market Context**
   - Is the broad market down? (S&P 500, sector ETF performance)
   - Is this an isolated stock drop or sector-wide?
   - VIX level - is fear elevated or normal?
   - Recent market regime (bull, bear, choppy)

### Phase 2: Fundamental Analysis (Is This a Bargain or Trap?)

Run the **5-Point Bargain Test**:

#### Test 1: Is the Business Intact?
- **Moat preserved?** Did competitive advantages weaken or stay strong?
- **Revenue trajectory**: One-time miss or structural decline?
- **Margin structure**: Temporary compression or permanent erosion?
- **Balance sheet**: Still solid or stress emerging?

**Score**: 
- ✅ PASS: Business fundamentals unchanged, drop is sentiment/timing
- ⚠️ CAUTION: Minor fundamental deterioration, fixable
- ❌ FAIL: Structural business problem (declining TAM, disruption, moat loss)

#### Test 2: Is Management Credible?
- **Track record**: Have they navigated similar crises before?
- **Transparency**: Are they honest about the problem or spinning?
- **Action plan**: Do they have a credible fix, or just hope?
- **Insider activity**: Are insiders buying or selling into the drop?

**Score**:
- ✅ PASS: Management proven, candid, has plan, buying stock
- ⚠️ CAUTION: Mixed signals, uncertain execution
- ❌ FAIL: Management in denial, no plan, insiders selling

#### Test 3: Is the Valuation Compelling?
- **P/E, P/S, P/B vs history**: How cheap relative to company's own range?
- **P/E vs peers**: Discount justified by fundamentals or overblown?
- **DCF / intrinsic value**: What's the margin of safety?
- **Historical support levels**: Price bounced here before?

**Score**:
- ✅ PASS: Trading at 15-30% discount to fair value, historical low multiples
- ⚠️ CAUTION: 5-15% discount, modest value
- ❌ FAIL: Still expensive despite drop, or value trap (cheap for a reason)

#### Test 4: Is the Risk-Reward Favorable?
- **Upside case**: If problem resolves, what's upside? (+30-50%? +100%+?)
- **Downside case**: If problem worsens, what's downside? (-20%? -50%? -80%?)
- **Asymmetry ratio**: Upside/Downside (Target >2:1, ideally 3-5:1)
- **Probability-weighted**: Expected value positive?

**Score**:
- ✅ PASS: 3:1+ asymmetry, limited downside (-15-25%), large upside (+40%+)
- ⚠️ CAUTION: 1.5-2:1 asymmetry, moderate risk
- ❌ FAIL: <1:1 asymmetry, unlimited downside, capped upside

#### Test 5: Is There a Catalyst for Recovery?
- **Timing**: When could sentiment reverse? (Next earnings? Product launch? Macro shift?)
- **Catalyst clarity**: Is there a specific event or just "hope it gets better"?
- **Market attention**: Will anyone care when it works, or is it forgotten?

**Score**:
- ✅ PASS: Clear catalyst within 3-6 months, high probability
- ⚠️ CAUTION: Vague catalyst, 6-12 month horizon
- ❌ FAIL: No catalyst, "dead money" for years

#### Bargain Test Scorecard

| Passes (✅) | Verdict | Action |
|------------|---------|--------|
| 5 / 5 | **STRONG BUY** | Aggressive entry, large position (5-10% portfolio) |
| 4 / 5 | **BUY** | Standard entry, normal position (3-5% portfolio) |
| 3 / 5 | **WATCH** | Small starter position (1-2%), monitor closely |
| 2 / 5 | **AVOID** | Do not buy, likely value trap |
| 0-1 / 5 | **SELL** | If you own, consider exiting |

### Phase 3: Entry Strategy (When & How Much to Buy)

#### Entry Timing

**Don't Try to Catch the Falling Knife**
- Wait for signs of stabilization:
  - Price stops making new lows for 2-3 days
  - Volume decreases (panic selling exhausted)
  - Positive divergence (RSI, MACD making higher lows while price makes lower lows)
  - Reclaim key technical level (50-day MA, prior support)

#### Key Support Levels (Technical Entry Zones)

Support levels are price zones where buying pressure historically overwhelms selling pressure, creating "floors" that can bounce prices. Use these to determine optimal entry prices.

**Types of Support Levels**:

1. **Psychological Support** (Round Numbers)
   - Humans anchor to round numbers ($50, $100, $200)
   - Example: Stock drops from $115 to $102, likely support at $100
   - Strong at decade marks ($50, $100, $150) and half-decades ($75, $125)

2. **Moving Average Support**
   - **50-day MA**: Short-term support, tested in healthy uptrends
   - **200-day MA**: Long-term support, breaking this = bearish
   - **20-day MA**: Very short-term, use only for strong momentum stocks
   - Example: Stock drops to 50-day MA ($85) → often bounces there

3. **Prior Swing Lows/Highs**
   - Previous low points that held (e.g., 6-month low was $78, now revisiting)
   - Previous resistance becomes support (stock broke out above $90, now $90 is support)
   - Look back 3-12 months for relevant levels

4. **Fibonacci Retracement Levels**
   - Common retracement levels: 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% from recent high to low
   - Example: Stock rallied $60→$100, Fib levels at $85 (38.2%), $80 (50%), $75 (61.8%)
   - 50% retracement is most common "healthy pullback" level

5. **Volume Profile Support** (High Volume Nodes)
   - Price zones where heavy volume traded in the past
   - Indicates "value area" where buyers/sellers agree on price
   - Use trading platforms (TradingView, ThinkorSwim) to identify

6. **Gap Fill Support**
   - If stock gapped up (e.g., $80→$90 overnight), gap at $80-90 often gets "filled"
   - Gaps act as support once filled
   - Not all gaps fill, but common enough to watch

**How to Identify Key Support for Dip Buying**:

**Step 1: Chart the Stock** (6-12 month view)
- Mark all prior swing lows (local bottoms)
- Mark 50-day and 200-day moving averages
- Note psychological levels ($50, $100, etc.)

**Step 2: Rank Support by Strength**
- **Strong support** = 3+ factors align (e.g., $80 = 50-day MA + prior low + psychological)
- **Moderate support** = 2 factors (e.g., $75 = Fib 61.8% + volume node)
- **Weak support** = 1 factor (e.g., $70 = just a round number)

**Step 3: Set Buy Zones at Strong Support Levels**
- Entry 1: At first strong support below current price
- Entry 2: At second strong support (deeper value)
- Entry 3: At third strong support (extreme oversold)

**Example: SMH Support Analysis**

Current price: $583, recent high: $643

**Identified Support Levels**:
1. **$580** = 20-day MA (current) - Weak support (just tested)
2. **$550** = Psychological + prior swing low (May 2026) - **STRONG** ⭐
3. **$507** = 50-day MA - **STRONG** ⭐ (primary support)
4. **$480** = Fib 50% retracement + volume node - **MODERATE**
5. **$430** = Fib 61.8% + prior low (Mar 2026) - **STRONG** ⭐
6. **$393** = 25th percentile (lower quartile) - MODERATE
7. **$337** = 52-week low - **STRONG** ⭐ (last resort)

**Entry Plan Based on Support**:
- Entry 1 (33%): **$540-550** (wait for break of $580, bounce at $550)
- Entry 2 (33%): **$500-510** (50-day MA support)
- Entry 3 (33%): **$425-435** (deep Fib support, only if thesis remains intact)

**Why This Works**:
- Buying at support = better risk-reward (less downside, more upside)
- Support failures become stop-losses (e.g., if $507 breaks, exit)
- Psychological edge: Buying at "consensus value" reduces FOMO/panic

**Entry Triggers** (Use 1-3 of these):

1. **Support-based**: Stock falls to key support level (e.g., "buy at $75-80 near 50-day MA")
2. **Time-based**: Wait X days after catalyst for stabilization (e.g., "buy 3-5 days after earnings miss")
3. **Technical**: Reclaims 50-day MA or breaks above downtrend line
4. **Fundamental**: Management announces credible action plan
5. **Insider**: CEO/CFO makes open-market purchase (not scheduled)

**Bad Entry Triggers** (Avoid these):
- ❌ "It's down 20%, must be cheap!" (Recency bias)
- ❌ "I'll buy at the bottom" (Impossible to time, leads to catching knives)
- ❌ "Everyone is bearish, time to buy!" (Contrarian for sake of being contrarian)

#### Position Sizing

**The 1/3 - 1/3 - 1/3 Rule** (For 3-5% target position):

**First Buy (33% of planned position)**:
- **When**: Price has fallen -15-25%, shows initial stabilization
- **Size**: 1-2% of portfolio
- **Purpose**: Get exposure, test the thesis, avoid FOMO

**Second Buy (33% of planned position)**:
- **When**: Either:
  - **(A) Bounce scenario**: Price rallies +8-12% from low, confirms reversal
  - **(B) Continued drop scenario**: Price falls another -10-15%, deepens discount
- **Size**: 1-2% of portfolio
- **Purpose**: Average in, reduce risk of all-at-once error

**Third Buy (33% of planned position)**:
- **When**: Either:
  - **(A) Confirmation**: Catalyst plays out (earnings beat, new CEO, etc.)
  - **(B) Deep value**: Price hits absurd discount (40-50% below fair value)
- **Size**: 1-2% of portfolio
- **Purpose**: Maximize position if thesis validates, or capitalize on extreme fear

**Position Sizing Guardrails**:
- **Max position**: 10% of portfolio (even if you love it - diversification matters)
- **Max sector exposure**: 25% of portfolio (don't bet everything on one sector)
- **Cash reserve**: Keep 20-30% cash during volatile markets (dry powder for more opportunities)
- **Risk-adjusted sizing**: Higher conviction = larger size, but never exceed 10% single name

**Alternative: The Pyramid Approach** (More aggressive):

```
Entry 1: 20% of position at -15% drop
Entry 2: 30% of position at -25% drop
Entry 3: 50% of position at -35% drop (if it gets there)
```

This "inverts the pyramid" - buy MORE as it gets cheaper (if thesis remains intact). **Risky** - only use if:
1. Balance sheet is fortress (no bankruptcy risk)
2. You have HIGH conviction (5/5 on Bargain Test)
3. You have capital to deploy (not already overexposed)

### Phase 4: Managing Continued Drops (It Keeps Falling - Now What?)

#### Decision Framework

**First, Reassess the Thesis** (Re-run Bargain Test):
- Did new information emerge? (New earnings, management change, competitive threat?)
- Are fundamentals deteriorating or just sentiment?
- Is the market overreacting or seeing something you missed?

**Three Scenarios**:

**Scenario A: Thesis Intact, No New Negatives**
- **Action**: **Average down** (buy more at lower prices)
- **Conditions**:
  - Business fundamentals unchanged (revenue, margins, moat stable)
  - Drop is purely sentiment (sector rotation, macro fear, technical selling)
  - Valuation now even more compelling (30-50% below intrinsic value)
  - You still have capital to deploy (<5% portfolio position currently)
- **Execution**:
  - Add 1-2% position size every -10-15% drop
  - Max position size: 10% portfolio
  - Keep averaging down until thesis breaks OR position limit hit
- **Example**: Stock drops -30% on tariff fears, but company has no China exposure. Fundamentals unchanged. Buy more.

**Scenario B: Mixed Signals, Uncertainty Increasing**
- **Action**: **Hold, Do Nothing** (wait for clarity)
- **Conditions**:
  - New information is ambiguous (could be bad, could be overblown)
  - Thesis is shaky but not broken (1-2 red flags, but 3+ green flags)
  - Price is falling but not accelerating (slow bleed, not waterfall)
- **Execution**:
  - Do NOT average down (don't throw good money after bad)
  - Do NOT sell (avoid panic selling at lows)
  - Wait for next catalyst (earnings, product launch, management comment)
  - Set mental stop-loss (e.g., "if it breaks below $50, I exit")
- **Example**: Stock drops on vague "accounting review" - could be minor, could be fraud. Wait for details.

**Scenario C: Thesis Broken, Fundamentals Deteriorating**
- **Action**: **Exit** (cut losses, move on)
- **Conditions**:
  - Revenue declining structurally (not one-time miss, but trend)
  - Moat eroding (competitors winning, pricing power lost)
  - Management in denial or incompetent
  - Fails 3+ tests on Bargain Test (score 2/5 or worse)
- **Execution**:
  - Sell 50-100% immediately (depending on severity)
  - If you want to salvage something, keep 1% "lottery ticket" position
  - Accept the loss, learn from it, move on (sunk cost fallacy kills returns)
- **Example**: Stock drops -40% because AWS launches competing product at 1/10th the price. Moat destroyed. Sell.

#### Stop-Loss Rules

**Time-Based Stop**: "If thesis doesn't play out in 12-18 months, exit"
- Purpose: Avoid dead money, opportunity cost
- Exit trigger: No progress on catalyst, price flat/down after 18 months

**Price-Based Stop**: "If price falls -X% from entry, exit"
- **Soft stop** (re-evaluate): -25-30% from average cost
- **Hard stop** (auto-sell): -40-50% from average cost
- Adjust based on volatility (tech stocks: wider stops, utilities: tighter stops)

**Fundamental Stop**: "If X happens, exit immediately"
- Examples:
  - Dividend cut (for dividend stocks)
  - Credit rating downgrade to junk (for stable value plays)
  - CEO resigns abruptly (governance red flag)
  - Accounting restatement (fraud risk)

**Portfolio-Level Stop**: "If this position becomes >15% of portfolio (due to other losses), trim"
- Purpose: Don't let one position dominate (concentration risk)
- Action: Rebalance to 10% max

### Phase 5: Managing the Bounce (It's Recovering - Now What?)

#### Decision Framework

**First, Assess the Bounce**:
- **Size**: +5-10% (noise) vs +20-30% (real reversal) vs +50%+ (home run)
- **Speed**: Slow grind or sharp V-shaped recovery?
- **Volume**: Bouncing on heavy volume (conviction) or light volume (short-covering)?
- **Catalyst**: Is the bounce driven by catalyst (earnings beat, deal announcement) or just oversold bounce?

**Three Scenarios**:

**Scenario A: Thesis Validated, Catalyst Played Out**
- **Action**: **Trim Profits** (take some risk off, let winners run)
- **Conditions**:
  - Stock rallies +30-50% from lows
  - Catalyst materialized (earnings beat, product success, activist campaign won)
  - Valuation no longer compelling (back to fair value or premium)
  - Position size is now 7-10% of portfolio (got big due to gains)
- **Execution**:
  - Sell 25-50% of position (lock in gains)
  - Move stop-loss to breakeven (protect principal)
  - Let remainder run (position is now "house money")
  - Re-evaluate: Is it still a hold at current price? If yes, keep 50%. If no, trim to 25%.
- **Example**: Stock drops from $100 to $60, you bought at $65. It rallies to $95. Thesis worked. Sell 30-40%, lock in 40-45% gain.

**Scenario B: Partial Bounce, Thesis Partially Validated**
- **Action**: **Hold Full Position** (let it play out)
- **Conditions**:
  - Stock rallies +15-25% from lows (recovering but not back to fair value)
  - Thesis is working but not complete (improving trends, but not home run yet)
  - Valuation still attractive (10-20% below fair value)
- **Execution**:
  - Hold 100% of position
  - Raise mental stop-loss to -10-15% from current price (protect gains)
  - Monitor for next catalyst (next earnings, product launch)
- **Example**: Stock drops from $100 to $60, you bought at $62. It rallies to $75. Good progress, but not at fair value ($85-90) yet. Hold.

**Scenario C: Dead Cat Bounce, Thesis Still Questionable**
- **Action**: **Sell on Strength** (use bounce as exit opportunity)
- **Conditions**:
  - Stock rallies +10-20% but fundamentals haven't improved
  - Bounce is technical (oversold relief rally) not fundamental
  - You re-ran Bargain Test, score is 2/5 (should have never bought, or thesis broke)
- **Execution**:
  - Sell 50-100% into the bounce
  - Don't wait for breakeven if thesis is broken (ego management kills returns)
  - Accept small loss (-5-15%) is better than large loss (-40-60%)
- **Example**: Stock drops -30% on earnings miss. Bounces +15% on short squeeze. Fundamentals still bad (guidance cut, margin compression). Sell into bounce.

#### Profit-Taking Rules

**The 3-Tier Exit**:

```
Tier 1 (25% of position): Sell when price hits 20-30% gain
- Purpose: Lock in quick win, cover position cost
- Execution: Automatic sell order at +25%

Tier 2 (50% of position): Sell when price hits fair value
- Purpose: Book most gains, reduce risk
- Execution: Sell when DCF / comps = current price

Tier 3 (25% of position): Let it run to bull case
- Purpose: Capture home runs (2-5x returns)
- Execution: Trailing stop-loss (20% from peak)
```

**Alternative: The "1% Rule"**:
- Sell 1% of position for every 5% gain
- At +50% gain, you've sold 10% of position
- At +100% gain, you've sold 20% of position
- Forces discipline, avoids all-or-nothing decisions

#### Re-Entry After Selling

**Scenario**: You sold at $95, stock dips back to $85. Re-buy?

**Decision Tree**:
1. **Is fair value still $100-110?** (Nothing changed fundamentally)
   - **YES** → Re-buy (you sold too early, price is still attractive)
   - **NO** → Don't re-buy (fundamental situation changed)

2. **Why did it dip?** 
   - **Profit-taking, sector rotation** (noise) → Re-buy
   - **New negative catalyst** (guidance cut, lawsuit) → Don't re-buy

3. **Ego check**: Are you re-buying because:
   - **Rational**: Price is attractive vs intrinsic value → OK to re-buy
   - **Emotional**: You regret selling, FOMO → Don't re-buy

**Rule of Thumb**: Only re-buy if you'd buy it TODAY having never owned it (ignore sunk costs, prior decisions)

## Position Management Summary

### The Complete Position Lifecycle

```
1. Initial Drop: -20% from high
   ├─ Run Bargain Test (5/5? 4/5? 3/5?)
   ├─ If 4-5/5: Enter 33% of planned position
   └─ If 2-3/5: Watch, do not enter

2. Position Entered (33% in, avg cost $80)
   ├─ Scenario A: Drops another -15% to $68
   │   ├─ Thesis intact? → Add 33% (avg down to $74)
   │   └─ Thesis broken? → Exit (cut losses)
   ├─ Scenario B: Bounces +12% to $90
   │   ├─ Thesis validated? → Add 33% (momentum)
   │   └─ Thesis uncertain? → Hold, wait
   └─ Scenario C: Flat, $78-82 for 3 months
       └─ Hold, wait for catalyst

3. Full Position In (100%, avg cost $78)
   ├─ Scenario A: Rallies to $105 (+35% gain)
   │   ├─ Sell 25% at +25% ($98)
   │   ├─ Sell 50% at fair value ($105)
   │   └─ Hold 25% with trailing stop
   ├─ Scenario B: Drops to $60 (-23% loss)
   │   ├─ Thesis intact → Hold or add (if <10% position)
   │   └─ Thesis broken → Exit (salvage $60)
   └─ Scenario C: Dead money at $75-80
       └─ Exit after 12-18 months (opportunity cost)
```

## Risk Management Rules

### Portfolio-Level Rules

1. **Max Single Position**: 10% of portfolio (even if high conviction)
2. **Max Sector Exposure**: 25% of portfolio (diversify across sectors)
3. **Cash Reserve**: 20-30% in volatile markets (dry powder for opportunities)
4. **Max Dip-Buying Allocation**: 40-50% of portfolio (don't put entire portfolio in fallen knives)
5. **Correlation Check**: Don't buy 5 tech stocks all down -30% (same risk, different names)

### Position-Level Rules

1. **Stop-Loss**: -40-50% max loss per position (hard stop, no exceptions)
2. **Time Stop**: Exit if no progress in 12-18 months (dead money rule)
3. **Averaging Down Limit**: Max 3 tranches (don't keep throwing money at losers)
4. **Profit-Taking**: Sell at least 25% at +30% gain (lock in wins)
5. **Rebalancing**: Trim positions that grow >10% of portfolio

### Emotional Discipline Rules

1. **Sunk Cost Fallacy**: "I'm down -50%, can't sell now" ← WRONG. If thesis broken, exit at -50% is better than -80%.
2. **Anchoring Bias**: "It was $100, now $60, must go back to $100" ← WRONG. Price has no memory. What's it worth TODAY?
3. **Confirmation Bias**: Seeking only bullish news ← WRONG. Actively seek disconfirming evidence.
4. **Loss Aversion**: Holding losers forever, selling winners too early ← WRONG. Let winners run, cut losers quickly.
5. **FOMO**: Buying just because it's down, not because it's cheap ← WRONG. Bargain Test must pass.

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

**The "It's Down, Must Be Cheap" Fallacy**:
- ❌ WRONG: "Stock is down -30%, time to buy!"
- ✅ RIGHT: "Stock is down -30%. Is it a bargain or value trap? Run Bargain Test."

**The "Average Down Blindly" Trap**:
- ❌ WRONG: Keep buying as it falls -20%, -30%, -50% (doubling down on losers)
- ✅ RIGHT: Average down ONLY if thesis intact AND position <10% AND you have capital

**The "Wait for the Bottom" Mistake**:
- ❌ WRONG: "I'll wait for the exact bottom before buying"
- ✅ RIGHT: "I'll buy when it shows stabilization, even if it's 5-10% off the low"

**The "Sell at Breakeven" Error**:
- ❌ WRONG: "I'll sell when I get back to breakeven" (ego management)
- ✅ RIGHT: "I'll sell when thesis is validated (profit) or broken (loss), not at breakeven"

**The "Revenge Trading" Impulse**:
- ❌ WRONG: Stock drops, you panic sell at low, then FOMO buy back higher (whipsaw)
- ✅ RIGHT: If you sell, accept it. Don't re-buy out of regret. Only re-buy if thesis re-validates.

**The "Set and Forget" Laziness**:
- ❌ WRONG: Buy and ignore for years (hope it works out)
- ✅ RIGHT: Monitor quarterly, reassess thesis, trim/add/exit as situation evolves

## Key Principles

1. **Distinguish Bargain from Value Trap**: Falling price ≠ good investment. Run Bargain Test.
2. **Enter in Stages**: 1/3 - 1/3 - 1/3 (or pyramid). Don't go all-in at once.
3. **Average Down Only If Thesis Intact**: Don't throw good money after bad.
4. **Lock in Gains**: Sell 25-50% at +30-50% profit. Let rest run.
5. **Cut Losers Quickly**: If thesis breaks, exit. Don't wait for bounce.
6. **Time Limit**: If no progress in 12-18 months, exit (opportunity cost).
7. **Position Limits**: Max 10% single position, 25% sector, 50% dip-buying total.
8. **Emotional Discipline**: No FOMO, no revenge trading, no sunk cost fallacy.
